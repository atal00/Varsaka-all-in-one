This isn't a "you should always outsource" pitch. Sometimes hiring in-house is right. Here's how to tell.

## The honest trade-offs

**In-house QA** gives you deep product context, instant availability, and a teammate who owns quality culturally. But one hire is a single point of failure, brings a narrow tool set, and costs a full salary + ramp time before you know if QA is even your bottleneck.

**Outsourced QA** gives you a senior team across functional, automation, performance, and security on day one, flexes with your release cadence, and costs nothing when you don't need it. The trade: you have to communicate context deliberately rather than by osmosis.

## A simple framework

Ask three questions:

1. **Is QA your steady-state bottleneck, or a spiky need?** Spiky (pre-launch surges, a security audit, a regression backlog) → outsource. Steady and central → consider hiring.
2. **Do you need breadth or depth?** One person can't be expert in Playwright *and* JMeter *and* VAPT. If you need breadth now, a team beats a hire.
3. **Can you afford a wrong hire?** A mis-hire in a 6-person company is expensive. Outsourcing lets you validate what "good QA" looks like for you first.

## The common answer

Most early-stage teams are best served by **outsourcing first**, then hiring in-house once QA is clearly a full-time, central function — using the outsourced engagement to define the role they eventually hire for.
