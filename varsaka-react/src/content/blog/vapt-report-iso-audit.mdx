A vulnerability scan dump is not a VAPT report. If you're submitting one for an ISO 27001 or SOC 2 audit, here's what it actually needs to contain.

## The sections auditors look for

1. **Executive summary** — scope, dates, overall risk posture in plain language a non-technical auditor can act on.
2. **Methodology** — what standard you tested against (OWASP Top 10, etc.), tools used, and the testing window.
3. **Findings, each with:**
   - A **CVSS score** and severity rating
   - **Evidence** (request/response, screenshots) proving it's real
   - **Business impact** in concrete terms
   - **Remediation steps** specific enough to action
4. **Re-test results** — proof that remediated findings were verified fixed, not just reported.
5. **Scope statement** — exactly what was and wasn't in scope, so the auditor knows the boundaries.

## The detail that trips teams up

Auditors care about **CVSS scoring and remediation evidence**, not finding count. A report listing 40 "issues" with no severity, impact, or fix verification is worse than one showing 5 properly scored findings, all re-tested and closed. The story the report tells is: *we found risk, we understood it, we fixed it, we proved the fix.*

## Timing

Run VAPT early enough to **remediate and re-test before** the audit window — not the week before. Findings often need code changes, and the re-test is what auditors trust.
